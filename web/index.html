1. Normal-Form Game Formulation (Chicken / Hawk-Dove)

At an unsignalized intersection, vehicles approach simultaneously. Let $S_i = \{\text{Go}, \text{Yield}\}$. The payoff structure reflects:

  • Collision ($u = -100$): When both agents choose $(\text{Go}, \text{Go})$, resulting in severe physical and economic harm.
  • Proceed ($u = +10$): Immediate traversal without deceleration.
  • Yield ($u = +2$): Safe traversal with slight delay cost.
  • Deadlock ($u = 0$): Both yield, wasting intersection capacity.

\text{Mixed NE condition: } q \cdot u_A(\text{Go}, \text{Go}) + (1-q) \cdot u_A(\text{Go}, \text{Yield}) = q \cdot u_A(\text{Yield}, \text{Go}) + (1-q) \cdot u_A(\text{Yield}, \text{Yield})

2. Stackelberg Leader-Follower (Extensive Form)

When arrival times differ, the earlier vehicle commits to an action $a_L \in \{\text{Go}, \text{Yield}\}$. The follower observes $a_L$ and plays its best response $a_F^*(a_L) = \arg\max_{a_F} u_F(a_L, a_F)$.

Backward induction resolves the subgame-perfect equilibrium: Leader commits to Go knowing the rational follower will choose Yield. This eliminates the coordination failure of simultaneous games.

3. Bayesian Games with Driver Type Uncertainty

In mixed traffic, autonomous vehicles interact with human drivers whose risk profiles $\theta \in \{\theta_{\text{aggressive}}, \theta_{\text{cautious}}\}$ are private information. The AV maintains prior $P(\theta_{\text{agg}}) = p$. The Bayesian Nash Equilibrium yields a conservative yielding threshold for the AV to guarantee zero collision probability.

4. Vickrey Auction (Second-Price Sealed-Bid) Mechanism

A decentralized intersection controller invites bids $b_i$ corresponding to urgency $v_i$. The vehicle with the highest bid $b_{(1)}$ wins the right-of-way and pays the second-highest bid $p = b_{(2)}$.

Dominant-Strategy Truth-Telling: Bidding $b_i = v_i$ strictly maximizes expected utility, guaranteeing $100\%$ allocative efficiency without coordination delays.
